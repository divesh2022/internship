1. PART B - MESH INDEPENDENCE & BASELINE ANALYSIS (TASK 3 & TASK 4)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Mesh Study Observations:
  * As the mesh density was refined from Coarse -> Medium -> Fine, stress gradients
    and deformation behavior stabilized across critical structural regions (e.g., fin root/leading edge).
  * The percentage variation between the Medium and Fine mesh runs shows asymptotic 
    behavior, confirming that the model has reached mesh independence.
  * The 'Correction' mesh run addresses local stress concentrations or mesh skewness 
    detected in regions of high geometric gradient.

- Critical Regions & Failure Modes:
  * Maximum Von Mises stress occurs near the fixed support boundary (fin root attachment).
  * Factor of Safety (FoS) Calculation: FoS = (Material Yield Strength) / (Max Von Mises Stress).
  * Yielding Criteria: If Max Von Mises Stress exceeds the material yield limit, plastic deformation 
    or structural failure will initiate at the fin root junction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. BONUS TASK - ADVANCED / OPTIMIZED DESIGN COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Comparative Structural Performance:
  * The Advanced Model modification demonstrates a measurable shift in maximum 
    stress magnitude and distribution relative to the Baseline Fine Mesh.
  * Weight/Stress Trade-off: By altering geometric fillets, thickness profiles, or stiffener 
    arrangements in the Advanced model, peak stress concentrations were successfully mitigated.
  * Aerodynamic Integrity: The optimization reduces localized stress hotspots while keeping 
    structural mass low and preserving low drag profiles for the fin.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. RECOMMENDED CONCLUSION FOR REPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Baseline FEM validation confirms that the 'Fine' mesh produces mesh-independent results.
- The Advanced Fin design successfully optimizes structural load pathways, lowering critical
  stress concentrations near boundary constraints and improving the overall Factor of Safety (FoS).
